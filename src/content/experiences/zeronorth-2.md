After working close to vessel reporting, I moved into Voyage Optimisation — the part of ZeroNorth where maritime complexity becomes product decisions. The work sat in the messy and interesting space between weather, routes, fuel, commercial constraints, operational habits, and the simple fact that ships are expensive things to point in the wrong direction.

My role was to help build and evolve the software operators used to understand voyages and make better decisions. That meant working across React and TypeScript interfaces, backend services in .NET and NodeJS, cloud infrastructure, and the connective tissue that makes a platform reliable enough for real operational use.

The challenge was not just presenting data. It was shaping it into workflows people could trust: clear enough for daily decisions, flexible enough for edge cases, and robust enough to survive the realities of shipping. I contributed across product and platform boundaries, with a focus on maintainable architecture, useful abstractions, and keeping the system understandable as the domain grew.
