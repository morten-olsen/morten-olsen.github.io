I am a software engineer in Copenhagen with a suspiciously broad toolbelt and more than 15 years of professional hindsight. I started in the land of C# and server-rendered web apps, wandered into mobile when apps still felt like tiny miracles, spent years shaping frontend platforms, and eventually found myself building AI systems for the shipping industry. Apparently my career plan was "follow the interesting problems until the job title gives up."

The thread through all of it is architecture: not the diagram-on-a-wall kind, but the practical kind where code has seams, teams have room to move, and tomorrow's change does not require a small excavation crew. I like systems that are boring in production and expressive in development. I like abstractions that earn their keep. I like deleting cleverness when it starts charging rent.

Most of my career has had a frontend label on it, but I have a habit of crossing borders. If the problem lives in a Node service, a Terraform module, a React Native app, a Kubernetes cluster, or an agent prompt with too much confidence, I will probably go there. I have built mobile apps with millions of monthly sessions, cross-platform design systems for small teams, offline-first reporting tools for ships, voyage-optimization interfaces, and now agentic AI platforms where the codebase occasionally talks back.

Outside work, I run a homelab that is absolutely more complicated than necessary, self-host services for the joy of becoming my own support department, and manufacture side projects at a pace that suggests I have confused curiosity with time management. I write about the things I build, the mistakes I make, and the weird little lessons hiding in both — partly to share what I learn, partly so future me has a paper trail when something breaks.
