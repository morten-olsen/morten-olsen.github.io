# Biography Notebook

This folder is the working notebook for shaping Morten's personal/professional narrative.

The goal is to preserve the real source material from our conversation, then turn it into stronger written forms:

- a long, story-driven biography/about page with a curious maker tone
- shorter CV/profile summaries derived from the long version
- role-specific experience descriptions
- reusable phrases, metaphors, and positioning statements

## Structure

### `conversation-notes.md`
Chronological notes from the interview. This is closest to the raw material, but lightly cleaned for spelling and readability. Use this when resuming the conversation and checking what has already been said.

### `themes.md`
The emerging narrative themes. This is where repeated ideas are grouped across the conversation: tinkering, learning by rebuilding, productive abandonment, AI as workshop assistant, engineering as restriction, etc.

### `quotes-and-lines.md`
Promising phrases, candidate lines, and strong formulations. Some are direct user phrasing; others are assistant reflections that should be treated as drafts until approved.

### `open-questions.md`
Questions still worth exploring in later sessions. This keeps the interview conversational while making sure important gaps are not lost.

### `personality-and-voice.md`
Working notes about Morten's personality, communication style, and how to interpret what he says. Use this to keep the interview natural and to preserve his real voice rather than flattening it into generic CV language.

### Future files
As the material matures, add:

- `draft-long-bio.md` — Adam Savage-ish long-form narrative draft
- `draft-cv-summary.md` — short CV/profile versions
- `draft-experience-summaries.md` — role-specific polished summaries
- `source-facts.md` — verified factual claims, dates, roles, technologies, and constraints

## Working principles

1. Do not invent facts.
2. Separate source material from polished interpretation.
3. Preserve uncertainty; mark assumptions clearly.
4. Write the long version first, then derive short versions from it.
5. Voice target for the long version: curious, maker-minded, concrete, energetic, reflective, lightly funny — closer to Adam Savage explaining craft than a conventional LinkedIn bio.
