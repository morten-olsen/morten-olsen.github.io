# Personality and Voice Notes

This document captures observations about Morten's personality, communication style, and how to interpret what he says during the biography work.

These are working notes, not fixed judgments. Update them as the conversation continues.

## Core personality signals

### Tinkerer first

Morten seems to approach software primarily as a tinkerer and investigator. He is drawn to taking systems apart, rebuilding pieces, and understanding the hidden tradeoffs.

The result matters, but the process of discovery often matters more. A project can be valuable even if it is abandoned, because the learning remains.

### Curiosity-driven

Curiosity appears to be the main engine. New concepts or technologies often become personal workshops. If a technology is interesting, Morten may build something around it just to feel where the edges are.

This curiosity is not random; it is usually connected to understanding constraints, design decisions, and practical limitations.

### Pragmatic, not ego-driven

Even when Morten builds his own version of an established tool, the goal is not usually to prove he can do better. He often expects to return to the established solution after learning from the attempt.

This suggests a pragmatic humility: he values mature ecosystems, maintenance, shared understanding, and domain expertise.

### Strong exploratory energy

Morten is strongest in the early and middle phases of a project: finding the shape, proving the concept, making the core work, discovering the tradeoffs.

He is candid that the final polish phase takes more focus and willpower.

### Self-aware collaborator

Professionally, Morten has learned to work with people who complement his builder profile, especially people who enjoy finishing polish. This is an important leadership/seniority signal: he understands both his strengths and his limits.

## Communication style

### Conversational and associative

Morten does not seem to think in neat form answers. He builds meaning through conversation, examples, corrections, and refinement.

Avoid giving him long questionnaires. A better interview style is one thoughtful prompt at a time, then reflect back the emerging meaning.

### Conceptual but grounded

Morten often moves from concrete examples to abstract principles.

Example path:

- built infrastructure frameworks
- tried orchestration-like systems
- understood Terraform/Kubernetes-style tradeoffs
- broader principle: engineering is the art of restriction

When he gives an example, look for the underlying principle.

### Uses rough phrasing to reach precise ideas

There are typos and rough wording in the live conversation, but the underlying ideas are often precise and nuanced. Do not over-index on surface phrasing. Listen for the conceptual structure.

Example: "restricted enough to be usable but not so restricted as not being able to solve the task" is rough live phrasing, but it contains a strong design philosophy.

### Pushes back when the process is wrong

Morten corrected the process twice:

1. The assistant invented/fill-in-the-blanks instead of interviewing.
2. The assistant handed out a list of questions instead of having a natural conversation.

This means the process matters. He wants to co-create the story from real material, not receive generic polished copy.

### Values authenticity over polish

He wants strong writing, but not invented writing. The story should preserve the truth, including imperfect but human details such as abandoned projects, the 80/20 pattern, and the need for collaborators/AI in the finishing phase.

## How to interview Morten well

- Ask one natural question at a time.
- Reflect back what you heard before moving on.
- Separate interpretation from fact.
- Do not invent missing achievements or details.
- When a strong phrase appears, capture it, but verify before using publicly.
- Let stories emerge through examples rather than asking for a formal career summary.
- Expect themes to repeat; repetition is useful because it reveals the spine of the biography.

## How to interpret recurring ideas

### "I build it myself"

This usually means: "I need to understand the design space from the inside," not "I believe my implementation should replace the standard tool."

### "Abandoned projects"

This should not be framed simply as failure. Often these are experiments, field notes, or learning artifacts. However, there is also real tension: Morten wishes he finished more personal projects.

### "AI helps me finish"

This should not be framed as AI doing the thinking. It is more like a workshop assistant for the final 20%: glue, polish, repetition, completion, and making personal tools usable.

### "Engineering is restriction"

This appears to be a central philosophy. It means good engineering/product design is about shaping possibility so users can act effectively. It applies across UIs, APIs, infrastructure abstractions, and AI agents.

## Tone to aim for in final writing

- Curious
- Maker-minded
- Warmly self-aware
- Slightly funny
- Concrete
- Reflective
- Pragmatic
- Not corporate
- Not inflated

The long version should feel like a skilled craftsperson explaining why the underside of the object matters.

## Additional observation: careful about domain authority

When discussing maritime workflows, Morten explicitly noted that he is not an expert on the actual operational processes and is paraphrasing. This caution is important. In final writing, avoid making him sound like he is claiming domain expertise he does not claim.

Better framing: he understands the engineering/product shape of the problem and works with domain knowledge embedded in the platform, tools, data, and team.
